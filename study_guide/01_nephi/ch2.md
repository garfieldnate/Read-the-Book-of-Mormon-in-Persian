# 1 Nephi 2 — Persian Study Guide

## Intro

Chapter 2 of 1 Nephi follows directly from chapter 1: Lehi has received a dream in which the Lord warned him that the Jews seek his life, and he is now commanded to take his family and flee into the wilderness. In twenty-four verses the narrative moves through five episodes: the family's departure from Jerusalem southward to the Red Sea (vv. 1–6); Lehi's erection of a stone altar and sacrifice of thanksgiving (v. 7); his impassioned wish that Laman and Lemuel would be as faithful as the river and as constant as the valley (vv. 8–10); their murmuring explained and contrasted with young Nephi's prayer (vv. 11–18); and a personal revelation to Nephi that commissions him as future ruler and teacher — or warns that the brothers will become a scourge if they persist in rebellion (vv. 19–24).

Vocabulary spans six domains: **journey / wilderness** (بیابان، دریای سرخ، وادی، رود), **possessions / leaving** (دارایی، طلا، نقره، چادر), **sacrifice / worship** (قربانگاه، قربانی، پیشکش، سپاسگزاری), **commandment / obedience** (فرمان، مطیع، نگه داشتن), **murmuring / stubbornness** (غرولند، خیره سری، سخت دلی), and **revelation / promise** (نیایش، ایمان، برگزیدن، فرمانروایی). Grammar highlights unique to this chapter include the wishing construction _ای کاش + imperfect_ (vv. 9–10) and the conditional _تا جایی که + subjunctive_ (vv. 20–22).

Grammar: Possessive / pronominal suffixes

Persian attaches possessive clitics directly to nouns: _-am_ (my), _-at_ (thy), _-aš_ (his/her/its), _-mān_ (our), _-tān_ (your), _-šān_ (their). These are preferred over the longer `[noun] + [full pronoun]` construction.

> [Chapter summary](#chapter-summary): `خانواده اش`
> _xānevāde-aš_
> his family

> [Chapter summary](#chapter-summary): `پدرشان`
> _pedar-ešān_
> their father

The clitic attaches after the full noun phrase: `برادرانش` = "his brothers", `پسرانش` = "his sons", `پدرم` = "my father."

Grammar: Direct-object marker `را`

`را` marks a **specific or definite** direct object. It attaches to the end of the entire object phrase — after relative clauses, adjectives, or possessive-suffixed nouns.

> _ānhā dārāyī-ye xod-rā bāqī mī-gozārand_
> They leave their own possessions behind. [`را` follows the whole phrase `دارایی خود`]

> [Verse 4](#verse-4): `خانه اش را ترک کرد`
> _xāne-aš-rā tark kard_
> He left his house. [`را` follows the possessive-suffixed noun]

Grammar: Ezafe chains

The unstressed suffix _-e_ (spelled as a kasra ِ on consonant-final words, or as ی on ā/ū-final words) links a head noun to its modifiers: **head – modifier – modifier – …**

> [Chapter summary](#chapter-summary): `بیابانِ کنار{e} دریای سرخ`
> _bīyābān-e kenār-e daryā-ye sorx_
> the wilderness beside the Red Sea [three-link chain: wilderness ← beside ← sea-of-red]

The publisher rarely writes the kasra; this guide marks every editorial ezafe with `{e}` in accent-color so you can distinguish added from original.

Grammar: Imperfective `می-` and its negation `نمی-`

The prefix _mī-_ marks non-perfective aspect. Added to the present stem + endings → **present tense** ("he takes"). Added to the past stem + endings → **past habitual / continuous** ("he was taking / used to take"). Negation fuses: `نمی‌بَرد` "he does not take." In compound verbs `می-` attaches only to the light verb: `نیایش می‌کند` "prays", `باقی می‌گذارند` "they leave behind."

> [Chapter summary](#chapter-summary): `لیحای خانواده اش را … می بَرد`
> _Liḥāy xānevāde-aš-rā … mī-barad_
> Lehi takes his family …

Grammar: Archaic / biblical register

The translator deliberately chose high-register Persian to mirror the KJV-style English of the original. Key markers:

- `گفتا` (_goftā_) — archaic narrative past of `گفتن`, roughly "said he" (the `-ā` is a classical Persian narrative suffix).
- `آری` (_ārī_) — "yea, indeed"; literary equivalent of `بله`.
- `بنگرید` (_benegarīd_) — "behold!" (2pl imperative of `نگریستن`).
- `سرور` (_Sarvar_) — "master, chief" repurposed as "the Lord." Vocative `سرورا` [verse 19](#verse-19) uses the classical `-ā` ending.
- Bookish verbs over everyday equivalents: `آموختن` (not `یاد دادن`) for "to teach"; `نیایش کردن` (not `دعا کردن`) for "to pray."

> [Verse 9](#verse-9): `سخن گفت، گفتا: ای کاش تو هم می توانستی مانند این رودخانه باشی`
> _soxan goft, goftā: ey kāš to ham mī-tavānestī mānand-e īn rūdxāne bāšī_
> he spoke, saying: O that thou couldest be like unto this river.

- **قربانی** — _qorbānī_ — sacrifice, offering [Arabic]
  - _Etym_: from Arabic, root q-r-b "to approach, to be near"; verbal noun _qurbān_ "that which is brought near [to God]."

  - _Forms_: 3sg pres. `کند` _konad_; 3pl pres. `کنند` _konand_; past `کرد` _kard_; 3pl past `کردند` _kardand_; neg. 3pl past `نکردند` _nakardand_ [verse 14](#verse-14); 1sg past `کردم` _kardam_; neg. 1sg past `نکردم` _nakardam_ [verse 16](#verse-16); pp. `کرده` _karde_; subj. 3sg `کند` _konad_ (`بـ-` dropped in compounds).

Grammar: Compound verbs (light-verb constructions)

Persian builds most of its verbs as **[noun or adjective] + [light verb]**. The meaning lives in the first element; the light verb (most often `کردن`, `شدن`, `دادن`, `گذاشتن`) handles inflection. In formal/literary Persian, compound-verb subjunctives **drop the `بـ-` prefix**: `نگه دارند` (not `*نگه بدارند`), `فرمانروایی کند` (not `*فرمانروایی بکند`).

> [Chapter summary](#chapter-summary): `با ایمان نیایش می کند`
> _bā īmān niyāyeš mī-konad_
> prays with faith [`نیایش کردن` = to pray]

Examples from this chapter: `نیایش کردن` (to pray), `غرولند کردن` (to murmur), `سپاسگزاری کردن` (to give thanks), `ترک کردن` (to leave/abandon), `باور کردن` (to believe), `زاری کردن` (to implore), `دیدار کردن` (to appear to/visit), `فرمانروایی کردن` (to reign).

Grammar: Passive voice with `شدن`

Persian forms the passive analytically: **[past participle] + شدن** (conjugated). The agent, if named, uses `از سوی` ("by the hand of") or `توسط`.

> [Chapter summary](#chapter-summary): `او برگزیده می شود`
> _ū bargozīde mī-šavad_
> He is chosen. [pp. `برگزیده` + present of `شدن`]

> [Verse 20](#verse-20): `کامروا خواهی شد`
> _kāmravā xāhī šod_
> thou wilt be prosperous. [future passive: `خواه-` auxiliary + pp.]

Grammar: `چنین گذشت که` — "And it came to pass that"

The most frequent syntactic frame in the Persian Book of Mormon. Literally "thus it passed that," it mirrors the Hebrew/English _wayyiqtol_ narrative formula. The main clause verb is always in the past tense.

Structure: `چنین گذشت که` + [narrative clause in past].

> [Verse 1](#verse-1): `چنین گذشت که سرور بر پدرم سخن گفت`

Grammar: `ای کاش` + imperfect — wishing construction

`ای کاش` (also written `ای کاشکی`) introduces an unfulfilled or unlikely wish: "O that …, would that …, if only …." The verb in the wish clause takes the **past imperfect** (می + past stem), not the subjunctive — a crucial distinction from purpose clauses.

> [Verse 9](#verse-9): `ای کاش تو هم می توانستی مانند این رودخانه باشی پیوسته روانه`
> _ey kāš to ham mī-tavānestī mānand-e īn rūdxāne bāšī pīvaste ravāne_
> O that thou mightest be like unto this river, continually running. [past impf. `می توانستی` + subjunctive complement `باشی`]

> [Verse 10](#verse-10): `ای کاش تو هم می توانستی مانند این وادی باشی، پایدار و استوار`
> _ey kāš to ham mī-tavānestī mānand-e īn vādī bāšī, pāydār o ostovār_
> O that thou mightest be like unto this valley, firm and steadfast.

The `ای` is a vocative/expressive particle; `کاش` is the wish particle (related to Arabic _kāšku_). Together they form a fixed phrase. The embedded subjunctive `باشی` follows the modal complement `توانستی`.

Grammar: `تا جایی که` + subjunctive — conditional "inasmuch as"

`تا جایی که` (lit. "to the place/extent that") introduces a condition whose apodosis (result clause) takes the future `خواه-` + infinitive. The conditional verb is in the **present subjunctive**.

> [Verse 20](#verse-20): `تا جایی که تو فرمان های مرا نگاه داری، تو کامروا خواهی شد`
> _tā jāyī ke to farmān-hā-ye marā negāh dārī, to kāmravā xāhī šod_
> Inasmuch as thou shalt keep my commandments, thou shalt prosper.

> [Verse 22](#verse-22): `تا جایی که تو فرمان های مرا نگاه داری، تو یک فرمانروا خواهی بود`
> _tā jāyī ke to farmān-hā-ye marā negāh dārī, to yek farmānravā xāhī būd_
> Inasmuch as thou shalt keep my commandments, thou shalt be a ruler.

## A final note on reading strategy

Chapter 2 is built on two opposing grammatical registers. The narrative framework — `چنین گذشت که` opening eleven verses — trains the reader to scan for that phrase as a scene-marker; once recognized, parsing the clause that follows becomes automatic. Within that frame, two constructions do the theological work: `ای کاش + imperfect` (vv. 9–10), which casts Lehi's wish for his sons as explicitly unfulfilled, and `تا جایی که + subjunctive / future` (vv. 20–22), which frames Nephi's commission as conditional rather than absolute. Mastering these three patterns — the narrative frame, the unfulfilled wish, and the conditional promise — gives you the skeleton of almost every scene in the book. As a study habit, read each `چنین گذشت که` clause and identify the verb in the trailing clause before moving to the vocabulary; that single step will double your reading speed across 1 Nephi.
