# Persian Noun Plurals

[TOC]

## Overview

Persian nouns carry no grammatical gender and mark only two number values: singular and plural. Several mechanisms produce plurals: the native suffix **-hā**, the formal suffix **-ān** (with allomorphs), and several Arabic-origin patterns — **broken plurals**, the **-āt / -jāt** suffix, and the **masculine sound plural -īn / -ūn**. In everyday speech -hā dominates; -ān and the Arabic-origin patterns belong mainly to formal writing and literary registers.

## Native Persian plural suffixes

### -hā (ها) — the universal suffix

**-hā** attaches to any noun regardless of meaning, animacy, or origin. It is the unmarked, default plural in both speech and writing, and the only productive suffix: new words — technical terms, foreign loans, neologisms — automatically take -hā.

| Singular | Plural | |
|----------|--------|-|
| `کتاب` *ketāb* | `کتاب‌ها` *ketāb-hā* | books |
| `دست` *dast* | `دست‌ها` *dast-hā* | hands |
| `آدم` *ādam* | `آدم‌ها` *ādam-hā* | people |
| `خانه` *xāne* | `خانه‌ها` *xāne-hā* | houses |
| `پسر` *pesar* | `پسرها` *pesar-hā* | sons, boys |

### -ān (ان) — the formal animate suffix

**-ān** is an Old Persian suffix that historically marked animate nouns, especially humans. In modern Persian its use is primarily literary and formal; colloquial speech almost always substitutes -hā even for human referents.

| Singular | Plural | |
|----------|--------|-|
| `مرد` *mard* | `مردان` *mardān* | men |
| `زن` *zan* | `زنان` *zanān* | women |
| `پادشاه` *pādešāh* | `پادشاهان` *pādešāhān* | kings |
| `پیامبر` *payāmbar* | `پیامبران` *payāmbarān* | prophets |
| `معلم` *moʿallem* | `معلمان` *moʿallemān* | teachers |

Some nouns strongly prefer -ān even in informal contexts — particularly kinship terms and titles with long literary histories: `پدران` *pedarān* (fathers), `مادران` *mādarān* (mothers), `فرزندان` *farzandān* (children/offspring). -ān also appears with some animal names in literary usage: `شیران` *šīrān* (lions), `اسبان` *asbān* (horses).

#### Allomorph -gān after final -e (ـه)

Nouns ending in the unstressed vowel *-e* (written ـه) drop the ـه and add **-gān**:

| Singular | Plural | |
|----------|--------|-|
| `بنده` *bande* | `بندگان` *bandagān* | servants |
| `فرشته` *ferešte* | `فرشتگان` *fereštagān* | angels |
| `زنده` *zende* | `زندگان` *zandagān* | the living |
| `مرده` *morde* | `مردگان` *mordagān* | the dead |
| `پرنده` *parande* | `پرندگان` *parandagān* | birds |

The ـه in these words traces back historically to the suffix *-ag*; dropping it before -ān leaves a connecting *-a-*, yielding the -agān sequence seen above. These plurals carry an elevated or literary tone; everyday speech prefers -hā (`فرشته‌ها`, `پرنده‌ها`).

#### Allomorph -yān after ā and u

Nouns ending in the long vowel *ā* and most nouns ending in *u* add **-yān** (the *y* acting as a glide to prevent vowel hiatus):

| Singular | Plural | |
|----------|--------|-|
| `خدا` *xodā* | `خدایان` *xodāyān* | gods |
| `دانشجو` *dānashjū* | `دانشجویان` *dānashjūyān* | students |

#### (Exceptions) Nouns ending in -u: -ān directly, no glide

A small set of nouns ending in *-u* take **-ān** without any connecting glide:

| Singular | Plural | |
|----------|--------|-|
| `بازو` *bāzu* | `بازوان` *bāzuān* | arms |
| `بانو` *bānu* | `بانوان` *bānuān* | ladies |

These are exceptions to the -yān pattern and must be memorized individually.

## After numerals: the singular form

When a noun follows a numeral or a quantity word (`چند` *čand* "a few", `هر` *har* "every", `هیچ` *hīč* "no"), the noun takes the **singular** form — no plural suffix:

| Persian | |
|---------|-|
| `دو کتاب` *do ketāb* | two books |
| `سه پسر` *se pesar* | three sons |
| `چند روز` *čand rūz* | a few days |
| `هیچ کلمه‌ای` *hīč kalame-ī* | not a single word |

Adding -hā after a numeral is a colloquial intensifier ("all those X") rather than a neutral plural.

## Arabic loanwords

### -hā is always acceptable

Any Arabic loanword can take -hā. This is the colloquial norm and is never wrong:

`مسجدها` *masjed-hā* (mosques), `وقت‌ها` *vaqt-hā* (times), `اسم‌ها` *esm-hā* (names).

### Broken plurals (جمع مکسر)

Arabic nouns form plurals by internal vowel change — **جمع مکسر** *jamʿ-e maksar*, literally "broken plural." Persian inherited both the singular and plural forms of loanwords, so speakers learn them as vocabulary pairs rather than applying a living rule. Broken plurals are preferred in formal writing, religious and legal texts, and journalism; in speech -hā is freely substituted.

For more on Arabic borrowings and their consonant/vowel patterns, see the [Arabic borrowings page](arabic_borrowings.md).

The most common patterns in Persian vocabulary:

#### افعال (afʿāl) — very frequent

| Singular | Broken plural | |
|----------|---------------|-|
| `حکم` *ḥokm* | `احکام` *aḥkām* | rulings, decrees |
| `شکل` *šakl* | `اشکال` *aškāl* | forms, shapes |
| `نوع` *nowʿ* | `انواع` *anvāʿ* | types, kinds |
| `وقت` *vaqt* | `اوقات` *owqāt* | times |
| `بیت` *beyt* | `ابیات` *abyāt* | verses of poetry |
| `سبب` *sabab* | `اسباب` *asbāb* | reasons, means |
| `فعل` *feʿl* | `افعال` *afʿāl* | verbs, actions |
| `اثر` *asar* | `آثار` *āsār* | works, traces |

#### فعول (fuʿūl) — frequent

| Singular | Broken plural | |
|----------|---------------|-|
| `علم` *ʿelm* | `علوم` *ʿolūm* | sciences, knowledge |
| `قلب` *qalb* | `قلوب` *qolūb* | hearts |
| `نفس` *nafs* | `نفوس` *nofūs* | souls |
| `امر` *amr* | `امور` *omūr* | matters, affairs |
| `رسم` *rasm* | `رسوم` *rosūm* | customs, traditions |

#### فعلاء / فعلا (fuʿalāʾ → fuʿalā) — for human nouns

Many Arabic *faʿīl* adjectives used as titles or roles take this pattern. The *-āʾ* ending drops in Persian:

| Singular | Broken plural | |
|----------|---------------|-|
| `عالم` *ʿālem* | `علما` *ʿolamā* | scholars, clergy |
| `امیر` *amīr* | `امرا` *omarā* | princes, commanders |
| `وزیر` *vazīr* | `وزرا` *vozarā* | ministers |
| `رئیس` *raʾīs* | `رؤسا` *roʾasā* | chiefs, heads |
| `نبی` *nabī* | `انبیا` *anbiyā* | prophets |

#### فواعل (fawāʿil)

| Singular | Broken plural | |
|----------|---------------|-|
| `حادثه` *ḥādese* | `حوادث` *ḥavādes* | events, incidents |
| `صنعت` *ṣanʿat* | `صنایع` *ṣanāyeʿ* | industries |

#### مفاعل (mafāʿil)

| Singular | Broken plural | |
|----------|---------------|-|
| `مسجد` *masjed* | `مساجد` *masājed* | mosques |
| `مسئله` *masʾale* | `مسائل` *masāʾel* | questions, problems |

### The Arabic -āt (ات) suffix and its allomorph -jāt (جات)

Persian has also borrowed the Arabic feminine sound plural **-āt**, especially for abstract nouns and verbal nouns (masdars). It is common in formal and journalistic writing:

| Singular | -āt plural | |
|----------|------------|-|
| `تحقیق` *taḥqīq* | `تحقیقات` *taḥqīqāt* | research, investigations |
| `اطلاع` *eṭṭelāʿ* | `اطلاعات` *eṭṭelāʿāt* | information |
| `ملاحظه` *molāḥeze* | `ملاحظات` *molāḥezāt* | observations, remarks |
| `مشکل` *moškel* | `مشکلات` *moškelāt* | problems |
| `تغییر` *taġyīr* | `تغییرات` *taġyīrāt* | changes |

When the base word ends in a vowel, the connective *-j-* is inserted before -āt, giving **-jāt**. This form is particularly common for categories of produce and goods:

| Singular | -jāt plural | |
|----------|-------------|-|
| `میوه` *mīve* | `میوه‌جات` *mīve-jāt* | fruits, produce |
| `تره` *tare* | `تره‌جات` *tare-jāt* | greens, herbs |
| `ترشی` *torši* | `ترشی‌جات` *torši-jāt* | pickles |
| `سبزی` *sabzī* | `سبزی‌جات` *sabzī-jāt* | vegetables |

-jāt plurals carry a collective or categorical sense ("produce in general", "vegetables as a category") rather than a count of individual items.

### Arabic masculine sound plural -īn / -ūn (ین / ون)

Arabic masculine active participles and a small set of adjectives carry the **masculine sound plural** (جمع مذکر سالم *jamʿ-e mozakkar-e sālem*): *-ūn* in the Arabic nominative, *-īn* in the genitive/accusative. Persian borrowed mainly the *-īn* form; *-ūn* survives in religious and Quranic contexts where Arabic case endings are preserved or imitated.

| Singular | -īn plural | -ūn plural | Gloss |
|----------|------------|------------|-------|
| `مسلم` *moslem* | `مسلمین` *moslemīn* | `مسلمون` *moslemūn* | Muslims |
| `مؤمن` *moʾmen* | `مؤمنین` *moʾmenīn* | `مؤمنون` *moʾmenūn* | believers |
| `کافر` *kāfer* | `کافرین` *kāferīn* | `کافرون` *kāferūn* | unbelievers |
| `مشرک` *mošrek* | `مشرکین` *mošrekīn* | `مشرکون` *mošrekūn* | polytheists |
| `صالح` *sāleḥ* | `صالحین` *sāleḥīn* | `صالحون` *sāleḥūn* | the righteous |

This pattern is restricted to Arabic-origin words with religious or moral content; it is not productive in Persian.

Note that the everyday Persian word for "Muslim" is `مسلمان` *mosalmān* — a Persianized variant in which -ān is fused into the word itself — rather than the Arabic form `مسلم` *moslem* shown in the table. Because `مسلمان` is already a Persian-pattern noun, it takes ordinary Persian plurals: `مسلمان‌ها` *mosalmān-hā* (colloquial) and `مسلمانان` *mosalmānān* (formal -ān plural). The Arabic -īn form `مسلمین` *moslemīn* applies only to the Arabic base `مسلم`, not to the Persianized `مسلمان`.

### When both forms coexist

For many words a broken plural and a -hā plural are both in use with slightly different register:

| Broken plural | -hā plural | Word | Note |
|---------------|------------|------|------|
| `مساجد` *masājed* | `مسجدها` *masjed-hā* | mosque | broken = formal/religious |
| `اوقات` *owqāt* | `وقت‌ها` *vaqt-hā* | time | broken = idiomatic in fixed phrases |
| `انواع` *anvāʿ* | `نوع‌ها` *nowʿ-hā* | type | broken = slightly more formal |
| `احکام` *aḥkām* | `حکم‌ها` *ḥokm-hā* | ruling | broken = religious/legal texts |

### Broken plurals repurposed as Persian mass nouns

Several Arabic broken plurals entered Persian as the **primary** form and are treated as uncountable mass nouns. The Arabic singulars they came from are still used in Persian, but often with a narrower scope or shifted register — the broken plural has taken over the dominant collective meaning:

| Form | Used in Persian as | Arabic singular |
|------|--------------------|-----------------|
| `اخبار` *axbār* | "news" (mass noun) | `خبر` *xabar* "a piece of news" |
| `اموال` *amvāl* | "property, wealth" | `مال` *māl* "possession, thing" |
| `احوال` *aḥvāl* | "affairs, how one is doing" | `حال` *ḥāl* "state, condition, feeling" |
| `اشعار` *ašʿār* | "poetry" (collective) | `شعر` *šeʿr* "a poem" |
| `ارواح` *arvāḥ* | "spirits" (collective) | `روح` *rūḥ* "spirit" |
| `علوم` *ʿolūm* | "learning, the sciences" (collective) | `علم` *ʿelm* "a branch of knowledge" |

When these forms are themselves pluralized in Persian, they take -hā (`اخبارها` "various news items"), though this is rare and sounds emphatic.

## Register summary

| Context | Preferred plural |
|---------|-----------------|
| Everyday speech | -hā for all nouns |
| Formal writing, journalism | -hā for Persian-origin words; broken plural or -āt for Arabic loans |
| Religious / legal texts | broken plurals strongly preferred |
| Literary / classical | -ān for animate nouns; broken plurals for Arabic loans |
| After numerals | no plural suffix |
